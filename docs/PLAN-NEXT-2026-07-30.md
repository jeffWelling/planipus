## 1. Five verified defects

Each was confirmed against code, not inferred from documentation. They order
everything below.

| # | Defect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **Presets redact content and publish rhythm.** `transform()` assigns `desired.timing = input.source.timing` verbatim; no preset touches timing. `busy_only` on a weekly 17:15 appointment publishes a perfect weekly 17:15 signal | `packages/calendar-sync/src/evaluate.ts:195` |
| **D2** | **"Never copied" is untrue of the database.** Under `busy_only`, the real title, description, location, attendee emails and organizer are persisted in unencrypted `jsonb`, and in every backup, while the disclosure preview says "never copied" | `server/migrations/0001_initial.sql:148`; label at `evaluate.ts:15-25,249` |
| **D3** | **Protect and Meet accept an activation that writes nothing.** The experimental planning gate is parsed server-side and emitted as `planning_writes_disabled`, with zero references anywhere in `web/src`. The browser accepts the activation, names a block count, and writes nothing to Google | `server/src/config.ts:122`, `server/src/planning/coordinator.ts:144` |
| **D4** | **The bridge wizard hard-codes six selection decisions, one of them wrong.** `all_day: 'skip'` is not a hidden option, it is a silent wrong answer — statutory closures, hospital days and all-day on-call rotations publish as *free* | `web/src/api.ts:408-446` |
| **D5** | **The Mac production Keychain path does not work.** `KeychainSecretStore` sets `kSecUseDataProtectionKeychain: true`, which on macOS requires an `application-identifier`/`keychain-access-groups` entitlement. There is no `Info.plist`, no `.entitlements` and no `.app` outside `.build`. A probe issuing exactly the `SecItemAdd` that `save()` issues, ad-hoc-signed and unbundled, returns **`-34018 errSecMissingEntitlement`**. `swift run PlanipusApp` cannot store the SQLCipher key or a refresh token today. The suite never sees it because `SecretStoreTests` uses `InMemorySecretStore` | measured on Darwin 25.5.0 |

D1 and D2 are disclosure failures in a product whose entire premise is
disclosure control. D3 is a safety defect — the fences in question are an ICU
charge nurse's post-nights rest before she drives home and a baker's sleep
before an oven shift. D5 means the Mac edition's encrypted-store work has never
run against the real Keychain.

## 2. What the personas actually asked for

Not features. Read `PERSONAS.md`'s deal-breaker lists rather than its feature
lists: seven of nine name D3, nine of nine are blocked by the absence of any
route to remove copies already created, and four have hand-deleted managed
events at midnight.

Eight of nine independently discovered D1 from unrelated threat models. Two
derived the correct mechanism themselves, and it is the one adopted here: **a
published fence must be a function of the policy alone, never of the events.**
Every variant that instead transforms real events — coarsening, rounding,
padding, jitter, merging — leaves the published shape a function of those
events, so an observer who samples across weeks recovers the signal by
differencing. Those are refused in §7.

## 3. The disclosure model

Three orthogonal, independently versioned axes. This is an evolution of the
existing preset registry, not a rewrite: the four content presets are unchanged,
the 91 conformance cases stay valid, and every presence tier is assembled from
primitives that already exist and already pass conformance.

**Content** (unchanged, v1) — `busy_only`, `commitment`, `private_details`,
`shared_details`.

**Presence** (new axis):

| Tier | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| `mirrored` | Today's behaviour. Naming it makes the choice explicit and makes the others describable |
| `absorbed` | **The concealment tier.** A Presence Fence — an `availability_boundary` rule whose shape comes from a named Hours profile *revision pinned at activation* — materializes private Busy blocks whose occurrence keys derive from hours intervals and from *no event*. The bridge then runs each source event through `evaluateHours` with `contained_in_profile` against that same pinned revision. Contained → `excluded()` with new reason `absorbed_by_fence`, so no copy is written, and because `excluded()` already deletes an attached copy, switching an existing bridge to absorbed cleans up prior copies through ordinary reconciliation. Not contained → fail closed to `held` with `absorption_gap`. **Never widen the fence to fit**: a fence that grows to fit an event is a fence shaped by that event |
| `suppressed` | A `SelectionPolicy` rule — an entire calendar marked never-bridgeable, or a deny-list on organiser domain or attendee identity, evaluated before the privacy transform. Fixes the structural hole where `#nosync` matches the source *title* and therefore fails for events the user does not own. Suppression must be invisible in Planipus's own reporting: no "N events excluded" count on any surface, which would reproduce the leak in a different font |

**Retention** (new axis): `content` (named and displayed, unchanged for detail
presets) · `timing_only` (verified computable: redacted presets never read
summary/description/location into the desired copy, and the only transient need
for content is the `#nosync` match, which reduces to a boolean at ingest) ·
`freebusy_only` (the existing availability role — the only privacy claim in the
product enforced by the *provider* rather than by trusting Planipus).

### What no tier can ever promise

1. Nothing hides from a Workspace admin, the Calendar audit log, or a
   Vault/eDiscovery export.
2. No tier makes an event both invisible and blocking. Reserving an interval *is*
   publishing that an interval is reserved. `absorbed` does not violate this: it
   publishes a window declared in advance and hides only which cycles are real.
3. No transform of real events defeats a long-horizon observer.
4. Readership of a calendar the user does not own is not enumerable. **"Readers
   unknown" is the honest answer** and is more useful than a partial list.
5. Disclosure is not recallable.
6. Client-side per-calendar default alerts are outside Planipus's control.
7. A Workspace admin can enumerate third-party OAuth grants. Planipus cannot hide
   that it is connected — say so before the first work-account consent.
8. Providers have no per-viewer visibility dimension.
9. Actor attribution is structurally unavailable against Google.

## 4. Sequencing

**The S1 directed-sync release gate is not displaced by anything in this
document.** Gate (a) — both editions against two live disposable Google accounts
across consent/scopes, create/update/move/resize/recurrence/delete, HTTP 410
recovery, quota, revoke/reconnect and identical-calendar-name — and gate (b) —
inspection of every privacy preset from an ordinary third Google viewer, with
owner visibility explicitly insufficient — remain the release-critical path. If
capacity is ever contested, the gates win.

### G0 — blocking everything

Commit the ~90-file working tree on `codex/mcp-auto-decline` in reviewable
slices, run the provenance gate, get a hosted CI run green, tag a base commit.
`main` has two commits; migrations 0007–0014 are untracked on disk. All release
evidence must be reported from an exact release SHA, and that cannot be produced
against a dirty tree. **Nothing else in this plan is verifiable until this
lands**, and every hour spent debating features compounds risk on work that
already exists.

Also delete `cmd/hourfold/` — an empty directory left from the pre-rename era.

### NEXT — ordered by dependency, not by demand

1. **The undo, destructive half** — previewed, counted, marker-verified removal;
   two-speed *stop and hide* vs. audited *destroy*; degraded outcome emitting the
   exact event list when the destination grant is gone; the audited purge that
   finally unblocks the availability-role downgrade.
2. **Hours profile CRUD + dated exceptions** — the highest value-to-cost ratio in
   the set. `hours.ts` implements all four exception kinds and DST resolution, 24
   conformance cases pin the behaviour, `hours_profiles` carries name and
   version, and the API already accepts `hours_profile_id`. What is missing is a
   REST route and a form: the wizard posts a fresh anonymous inline profile every
   time and planning rules bypass the table entirely. Rules must reference a
   **revision**, not copy a mutable window.
3. **CLI Phases 1–2** — shared `packages/api-client`, read-only CLI, agent
   envelope v1; then propose/apply on the already-bearer-reachable surface.
4. **Mac control plane Phases 1–3** — socket, enrolment, audit, content-free
   reads; then the disclosure lattice and ratification; then the Mac MCP adapter.
5. **Presence axis** as additive conformance v1.1 — `suppressed` first (needs no
   fence machinery), then Presence Fences and `contained_in_profile` absorption,
   with the matching Swift implementation so the canonical SHA-256 vector stays
   honest.
6. **`retention:timing_only`** for endpoints whose active policies are all
   redacted presets, with a migration decision for existing rows.
7. **Distribution** — signed multi-arch image, compose path, SBOM, provenance,
   metrics scrape scope, Google OAuth consent-screen documentation with a
   token-expiry warning, and a web app manifest.

Items 5 and the CLI's Phase 5 schema reconciliation are the **only** work that
can change directed-sync accept/reject behaviour. Both are gated on the 91-case
corpus passing unchanged before and after, with no case edits. A reconciliation
that requires editing a case is not a reconciliation — it is a behaviour change
and needs its own decision record.

### LATER

Multi-principal and OIDC, restricted to `busy_only` bridges plus
availability-only connections where `retention:timing_only` makes "the operator
cannot read content" structurally true rather than promised. Provider breadth,
and not before the gates: first *documentation* that Google Calendar can already
subscribe to an external ICS URL and that the resulting calendar can already be
bridged (free today, reaches a hospital roster and a PagerDuty feed, at a doubled
staleness that must be stated), then a Nextcloud-targeted CalDAV adapter under an
explicitly weaker trust label — because CalDAV has no equivalent of provider
private extended properties, so the ownership rule would lose one of its two
co-authorities. Accessibility and responsive conformance on Protect and Meet.

## 5. Why NOW looks unglamorous

It fixes an untrue label, stops a false success, corrects a silent all-day
defect, repairs a Mac Keychain path that has never worked, and gives users a
screen showing what the tool has done to their calendars.

The reasoning is that a user who catches the product overstating once will not
run the live-Google tests the release needs from them. The three personas most
able to generate real-world evidence are also the three most likely to read the
source and find D3 before they find the feature list.

## 6. Surfaces

`SURFACES.md` is authoritative. Summary of what changes:

- One operation registry; thin surfaces generated, thick surfaces checked by AST
  extractor, split by blast radius.
- `PARITY_DEBT` must be exactly 0, asserted per edition.
- `SESSION_ONLY_WEB_OPS` is **15** today, must never increase, and drops to **3**
  after a reviewed promotion PR — the api-token lifecycle stays browser-only
  forever.
- `--agent` emits minified JSON, one document, one newline, stable key order, no
  prose, no ANSI, ids not labels, RFC 3339 UTC. `--json` is deliberately *not* an
  alias.
- Exit code **11** (`api_timeout_outcome_unknown`) is distinct from 10 so a retry
  loop cannot double-activate a bridge or send a second round of cancellations.
- Agent-profile ceilings default to `max_deletes 0` and
  `max_attendee_invites 0`; attendee-visible operations need a flag with **no
  environment-variable equivalent**.

## 7. Deliberate non-goals

Refused with reasons, so they stop being re-litigated.

- **Rostering and availability publication.** Planipus publishes *busy*, not
  *availability*. Every managed copy is bound to exactly one source observation
  through ownership markers, `source_basis_hash`, generation and
  `intent_sequence`; a complement-publication has no source to anchor to, and it
  asserts "free" about calendars Planipus was never granted.
- **Coverage and dependent modelling.** Requires a domain object that is a person
  rather than a calendar account, an eligibility model and a solver. Say so
  directly, because a tool that is vague about this will be trusted to catch a
  daycare-door failure it cannot catch.
- **Turnaround and minimum-gap constraints.** Hours are per-occurrence wall-clock
  windows; a gap constraint is relational between events, needing a new
  evaluation stage, new cases and Swift parity.
- **Non-weekly rotation as a contract change.** `weekly_intervals` is keyed on
  weekday 1–7 across the schema, both evaluators and 24 frozen cases. Once Hours
  CRUD exists, a 2-2-3 rotation is generated dated exceptions over a bounded
  horizon — exceptions the contract already handles.
- **Merging and jitter as cadence protection.** Merging destroys the 1:1
  projection relation; jitter makes `desired_fingerprint` unstable and fights the
  verifier. More importantly, neither works.
- **Decoy blocks.** Only useful if indistinguishable from real ones, which
  requires the provenance markers that make Planipus trustworthy to be invisible
  — and they are separable anyway by `created`/`updated` timestamps and the
  domain audit log. The defensible half is adopted: a managed copy must be
  visually and structurally indistinguishable from an ordinary user-created
  private block, with the user supplying the generic label.
- **Tamper-evident legal-evidence export**, and the words *immutable*,
  *tamper-proof* and *court-ready* anywhere in the product. Hash-chaining that a
  self-hosting operator can also rewrite is not tamper-evidence against the party
  a court would ask about. What ships instead is a plain export worded strictly
  as "what this tool wrote", never "what was visible".
- **Actor attribution.** Structurally unanswerable against Google.
- **Time-tracking, billable classification, relationship-fairness ledgers.** Each
  requires persisting classified event content over time — the exact discipline
  the no-copy domain exists to avoid.
- **Push notifications and native mobile.** The notification is itself a
  disclosure surface: a lock-screen banner naming a bridge is the leak the
  product exists to prevent.
- **Per-viewer visibility.** No provider has a viewer dimension. Four audiences
  means four destination calendars and four bridges, and the UI must say so.
- **Remote MCP over Streamable HTTP.** Restated: the stdio process must never be
  proxied and described as remote MCP.
- **Server feature parity by proxy on the Mac.** Bringing Protect, Meet or
  no-copy replies to the Mac requires native Swift implementations, Mac-specific
  fixtures, security review and independent live evidence — never a call to a
  Server.

## 8. Decisions the owner owns

Recorded as options with a recommendation, not as questions.

1. **Google OAuth client identity.** A university and two client Workspaces in
   the persona set enforce Marketplace allowlists, so an unrecognised client
   fails with `admin_policy_enforced` before any feature matters. (a) publish a
   verified client, requiring CASA assessment and a named publisher; (b) BYO-only;
   (c) both, stating in the UI which is in use. **Recommend (c) with (b) as
   default.** Gates distribution and cannot be deferred past the first packaged
   release.
2. **Default retention for redacted presets.** **Recommend default-on** for
   endpoints whose active policies are all `busy_only`/`commitment`, plus a
   deployment-wide config so a self-hoster can enforce it organisation-wide.
3. **Distribution policy for the first image.** Supply-chain gates are genuinely
   unmet. **Recommend publishing an explicitly-labelled unreviewed pre-release**,
   since the alternative is that the adopters most able to generate real evidence
   cannot run the product at all.
4. **Bridge retirement default.** **Recommend forcing an explicit choice with no
   default**, detach offered first. This is the most dangerous operation in the
   product and the one most likely to be reached in a panic.
5. **Whether `presence:widened` ships at all.** **Recommend not for v1** — every
   persona who asked for coarsening actually wants `absorbed`, and a tier whose
   main effect is to be misunderstood costs more than it returns.
6. **Multi-principal scope.** **Recommend the restricted form** (`busy_only` plus
   availability-only only). It is the only option whose privacy claim survives
   contact with a members' meeting.
7. **CLI Phase 3 posture** — promote all twelve session-only routes, or keep the
   three attendee-visible ones browser-only forever?
8. **Mac enablement ceiling** — 24 hours maximum, or a documented 7-day option
   for users who explicitly accept the risk?
9. **Registry location** — `conformance/surface/v1/` beside the behaviour
   fixtures, or `registry/v1/` at the root?

## 9. Verification owed before any of this is believed

- Clean-machine check that a terminal with no TCC grants can `connect()` into the
  app container socket. The reachability test that informed ADR-006 ran from a
  shell holding Full Disk Access, which is a real confound. If it fails, the
  fallback is a Group Container path.
- Whether `extendedProperties.private` markers are readable by an ordinary third
  viewer on a shared calendar. If they are, that is a disclosure channel that
  independently affects the presence axis.
